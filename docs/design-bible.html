UNDERSONG

A mining village digs toward a song coming from under the mountain, and finds out it has dug here before.

Key art · rendered live by the same tile and lighting rules the game will use

The pitch

Undersong is an incremental mining game told in side-on cross-section, in the pixel tradition of Terraria. You are the new foreman of Holloway, a village that hums on quiet nights. You dig, hire miners, haul ore, forge tools and light the dark. Every layer of rock holds a verse of a song nobody remembers learning.

When the mountain caves in, the village starts over, but you keep the verses. Each run digs a little deeper into the song and into what Holloway keeps choosing to forget.

The map is the game

Everything you build lives in one cross-section. Progress is something you can see and scroll.

Light is a resource

Darkness slows miners and invites pests. Placing light is the core layout puzzle.

The song is the story

Twelve verses, found in the rock, are the narrative spine. Nothing is told in cutscenes.

Every reset remembers

Prestige is part of the fiction. Each cave-in is the village forgetting, and you remembering.

Story bible

Premise

Holloway sits on the shoulder of a mountain that hums. The last foreman went down the shaft one night and didn’t come back, so the village sent for a new one. That’s you.

The deeper you dig, the clearer the hum becomes. It’s a song. Verses are carved into cave walls in handwriting that looks a lot like yours. Old tools carry the village’s mark from before the village existed. Further down there’s a drowned town laid out exactly like Holloway.

The truth is that Holloway has dug to the heart of this mountain many times. Each time, the song got too loud and the village caused a cave-in to forget it. Each new foreman is the last one, starting over.

The ending

At the Hollow Heart you can finally sing all twelve verses. You choose between two endings.

Seal the shaft

The village forgets one last time and the cycle continues, gently. This is a quiet, melancholy ending, and it unlocks Endless Depth.

Sing the last verse

Holloway remembers every cycle at once. The mountain opens to the sky. This is a hopeful ending, and it unlocks New Song+ with remixed verses.

Characters

The Foreman

You. Practical and new to Holloway. The verses are in your handwriting, and you don’t know why yet.

Old Wren

The lamplighter, who runs the Lamp-works. She knows more than she says, and her dialogue changes each cycle.

Pell

A child who hums verses before you find them. Pell is the player’s hint system and the story’s heart.

Bram

The smith, who runs the Forge. He’s dry and funny, and he refuses to believe in songs.

How the story is delivered

Verses

Twelve carvings hidden in the rock, one or more per biome. Finding one plays its line of the song and unlocks a story beat.

The Survey Book

The prestige screen is a ledger. Every cave-in adds an entry in your handwriting, and early entries were written by foremen before you.

Village lines

Villagers say one short line on the surface, and their lines change with depth, with verses found and with each cycle.

“Down where the lanterns forget their light, / the stone remembers, the stone holds tight…”

Game systems

Core loop

Dig rock to expose ore. Haul it up the shaft. Smelt it into bars at the Forge. Craft tools, rails and lanterns. Light the new tunnels so miners work at full speed. Descend to harder rock and richer ore.

Verses sit off the main path, so finding them rewards exploring the map rather than racing straight down.

Active play

  • Hand-mining. Click or tap a tile to mine it, and hold to keep mining. The foreman is always faster than a miner.
  • Vein Rush. Mining connected ore tiles quickly builds a multiplier up to ×5.
  • Chests and Glints. Timed finds that appear near the active foreman and give bursts of resources.
  • Pests. Clicking them off quickly stops them stalling miners in dark tunnels.

Idle play

  • Miners work assigned faces on their own, at a speed set by light and rock hardness.
  • Logistics carry ore up automatically, and throughput is the real cap.
  • Offline progress covers 8 hours at 50% efficiency. Echo upgrades raise that to 24 hours at 100%.
  • “While you were away” is told as a short verse-styled summary.

Systems

System What it does Unlocks
Miners Hired at the Bunkhouse and assigned to a rock face. Output depends on pick tier, light and hardness. Act I
Logistics Ore has to travel to the surface. Rope (1 tile/s), then lift (3), then rails (8), then song-lift (instant). Each shaft has a throughput cap. Acts I–III

Lamp-works Produces Lumen. Placed lanterns cost Lumen upkeep, so light becomes a budget. Act II

Prestige: the Cave-in

The Cave-in unlocks after reaching 300 ft and finding Verse II. The mountain collapses and gets a new seed, so the layout changes every run.

You keep

You lose
Depth, miners, buildings, ore and bars
You gain
Echoes, based on max depth and verses found this run

The Echo tree

Echoes are spent across three branches.

  • Hands covers mining speed, miner cost and Vein Rush.
  • Lamps covers light radius, Lumen and pest resistance.
  • Memory covers automation and story: starting with rails, auto-hiring, offline caps and verse hints from Pell.

Art direction

The look is chunky, readable pixel art with coloured light. Every asset is a text grid plus a palette, so new art always matches the old.

Grid & scale

  • Tiles are 16×16 and characters are 16×24.
  • Rendering uses integer scaling only (×2, ×3, ×4), with nearest-neighbour filtering and no anti-aliasing or rotation.
  • The camera snaps to whole pixels so tiles never shimmer.
  • Each material has four tile variants, chosen by a position hash so patterns never repeat in an obvious way.

Shading rules

  • Every material uses a three-tone ramp: base, light and dark.
  • Faces exposed to air get a highlight on top and left edges and a shadow on bottom and right edges.
  • Back walls use the same material at 42% brightness with less detail.
  • Sprites self-outline with the darkest colour in their ramp, never pure black.

Lighting

Light is calculated per tile, the way Terraria does it. There are two channels: warm (sky, torches, lanterns, ember) and cool (glowcaps, crystals). Light spreads from each source, losing 0.085 per air tile and 0.26 per solid tile.

Darkness is a near-black navy rather than true black, so silhouettes still read. Torches flicker by ±6%.

Type

Silkscreen is for numbers, labels and the HUD. Pixelify Sans is for verses, titles and dialogue. Body text in menus uses a readable bitmap-style face at 2× scale.

Motion & sound

Sprites animate at 8–12 fps, with 4-frame walks and 3-frame swings. Particles are single pixels with gravity. Music is a hummed lullaby on music box and low strings, adding one layer per biome so the song literally gets clearer as you descend.

Tech & asset pipeline

Stack

Language
TypeScript, built with Vite
World
Canvas 2D with integer scaling. The world is split into 32×32 chunks, each cached to an offscreen canvas and redrawn only when changed.
UI
DOM overlay using Preact for panels, menus and the Survey Book
Simulation
A fixed 10 Hz tick, separate from rendering and deterministic with seeded RNG
Numbers
break_eternity.js from day one, so late-game prestige never needs a migration
Saves
Compressed JSON in localStorage every 30 s, with export and import strings and versioned migrations
Hosting
Static deploy to Vercel, with an installable PWA for phones later

Responsive layout

  • Desktop, landscape. The cross-section sits on the left, with a side panel on the right for buildings, crafting and the Echo tree.
  • Mobile, portrait. The cross-section fills the screen and scrolls vertically. The hotbar and panels become a bottom tray, and tapping a tile mines it.
  • Tile scale steps between ×2 and ×4 at breakpoints, never fractional.
  • Lighting is recomputed per changed chunk with a flood fill, so it stays cheap on phones.

Asset pipeline

Each sprite is a plain text file. Every character is a palette key, so I can write, review and diff art like code. A build script packs everything into one atlas PNG and a JSON map, and the dev server hot-reloads on save.

New ores are palette swaps and new biomes are a palette plus generator rules, so the art stays consistent from the first tile to the four-hundredth.
